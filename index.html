“Too many comics” was never the problem.

I think I have too many comics. But my real problem was never the number. It was not having a good inventory of what I have, where it is, and what it's worth. If you're like me, Too Many Comics was made for you: point your camera at a stack and talk, and it catalogs every book, grades condition with AI, and keeps your whole collection in your pocket.

📸 Scan by camera and voice

Work through a long box hands-free: the camera spots each new cover, your voice adds the notes, and the AI fills in title, issue, printing, key-issue status, and more.

🔍 AI condition grading

Advanced Grading walks the checks a grader walks (spine, corners, gloss, pages) from your photographs, and prices at the honest end of the grade it can defend.

🔥 Hot Books

A daily sweep of comic-industry news, matched to the books you own. The matching happens on your phone, so nobody but you knows what's in your boxes.

🔒 Private by architecture

Your collection lives on your device. The backend sees one photograph at a time and keeps none of them. No account, no login, nothing to breach.

💰 Sell with your records straight

Mark books for sale, export eBay draft packs, and track what sold and for how much. It's your P&L, not your marketplace's.

⚡ Credits, not subscriptions

A weekly free allowance covers casual scanning. Buy credits when you need a big weekend. No recurring anything.

How does this all fit on my phone?

Because the facts about a comic are tiny, and the app is smart about the photos. A book's record (title, issue, grade, price, your comments, all of it) takes a few kilobytes. The photographs are where the space goes, so every cover is sized on the way to disk: sharp enough to see, search, zoom, and list on eBay, at a small fraction of the 12-megapixel original you'd never use.

<8 GBfor a 50,000-book collection, measured on an older iPhone. It works great, and it's your yardstick: roughly 1 GB per 6,000 books, at any collection size.
~5 KBper book of facts, measured on device. The photos are the space; the data is nearly free.
No capbuilt into the app. Your phone's storage is the only ceiling.

The philosophy: nobody buys a valuable book off a phone photograph. High-fidelity photos matter when the AI grades a book, so those are taken at grading time and kept at higher quality. For everything else, seeing and searching your whole collection beats museum-quality copies of it. Space saver mode is the default; a Storage screen in Settings shows exactly where your bytes go.
