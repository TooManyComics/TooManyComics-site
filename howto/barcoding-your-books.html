How To

Barcoding your books

Every book in your inventory already has a barcode. The app assigns one automatically the moment a book is recorded, so there is no numbering scheme to invent and nothing to keep in your head. Printing the labels and affixing them to the bags is the whole job, and once the books wear them, the collection stops being boxes you dig through and becomes something you can scan.

Print the labels

The app generates label sheets as a PDF for standard Avery stock, so you can print anywhere. Each label carries a QR code naming that exact copy (not the title, the copy: your Very Fine and your reading copy get different labels). Stick the label on the bag, never the book. The labels deliberately carry no price, because prices live in the app, where a re-price updates every book at once instead of costing you an evening of re-stickering.

Scan a Stack

Labels are what power Scan a Stack. Point the scanner at a pile of barcoded books and it reads them in seconds, tells you exactly which books they are and where they belong, and totals what the stack is worth. A customer's “what for these?” at a show, a pile that surfaced far from its box, a stack you are about to hand a friend: one sweep of the camera names them all. And it costs no credits, ever. A label scan is your own phone reading your own inventory, a local answer, always free.

Re-boxing without the ceremony

Labels also take the pain out of moving books. Select any set of books, by search or by scanning their barcodes, and change their box location in one move. Shuffling a long box into new homes becomes a scan and a tap, not an afternoon of editing records one at a time. The same labels drive Audit Box when it is time to check a box against the record; the accuracy guide covers that half.

See it in real life: a stack totals itself before the customer finishes the sentence in The stack and the offer, a label gets scanned straight off the kiosk screen in The convention table, and the same codes put the boxes back to truth in After the show.
