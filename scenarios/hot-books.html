The morning check

Buzz is what moves a book overnight

Nothing about a comic changes in its bag. What changes is the conversation around it. A casting announcement drops, a creator passes, a character turns up in a trailer nobody expected, and a book that was quietly worth forty dollars on Tuesday is worth a multiple of that by Thursday. Buzz is the mechanism. It always has been.

The old way to keep up was to be plugged in constantly, or to find out from the person buying your book that you had priced it before the news. Both are bad. And this is exactly the blind spot an AI price estimate cannot fix on its own, because a model knows what it was trained on, and it was not trained on this morning.

Hot Books is the answer to that. Every morning it sweeps a curated list of comic industry outlets, groups the stories that are really the same story, and maps them to the key issues they actually affect. Then it does the part that matters: it matches that news against the books you own and tells you what is buzzing in your own boxes, with new items badged since you last looked.

So the morning check answers two questions at once. What just got hot that I already have, and what just got hot that I should be hunting. If the news broke ten minutes ago and I want to look right now rather than wait for tomorrow's sweep, I can pay to run one on demand. If somebody else already checked the outlets a few minutes earlier, it tells me so and does not charge me for news that is already on the screen.

Behind it: the sweep is a curated allowlist of feeds that exist to be read, shown as headline, outlet and link rather than reproduced article text. The matching against your collection happens on your phone, so the feed is the same for everyone and nobody, us included, learns what is in your boxes. The first live sweep mapped real news to the right key issues on the first try: Avengers #9, Marvel Spotlight #32, Creatures on the Loose #10.
